Model and assumptions

Bulk EF comes from charge neutrality p − n + ND+ − NA− = 0 with Boltzmann carrier statistics and incomplete dopant ionization (degeneracy factors 2 for donors, 4 for acceptors), so the dopant choice matters for deep levels such as Mg in GaN. Moving the Fermi-level slider solves backward for the doping that gives it. Poisson's equation is integrated exactly in 1D from the interface into the bulk (no depletion approximation), with the dopant ionization following the local band bending. The displayed W uses the depletion approximation W = √(2ε(Vbi − V − kT/q)/qN).

Barrier: qφBn = S(qφm − qχ) + (1 − S)(Eg − qφ0) (Cowley–Sze), with φ0 an approximate charge-neutrality level for each material. When S < 1 the vacuum level steps at the interface (interface dipole) and the metal sheet charge in Fig. 3 includes the interface-state charge. By default minority carriers are held at their bulk density (the usual Schottky-diode picture). With the minority-carrier option on, at zero bias they follow Boltzmann statistics too, which can produce an inversion layer under large barriers. Under bias the majority-carrier quasi-Fermi level stays flat in the semiconductor and minority carriers stay at their bulk density. Image-force lowering is reported but not drawn. Transport regime uses E00 = (qħ/2)√(N/m*ε): thermionic below 0.5 kT, thermionic-field to 2 kT, field emission (tunneling) above. The I–V curve (Fig. 5) uses the Tsu–Esaki form J = A*T² ∫ T(E) ln[(1+e^{(E_F,s−E)/kT})/(1+e^{(E_F,m−E)/kT})] dE/kT, with a WKB transmission T(E) through the parabolic depletion-approximation barrier, so thermionic, thermionic-field and field emission come out of one calculation; the dashed curve is thermionic emission alone. A* = 120 m*/m₀ A cm⁻² K⁻². Image-force lowering is not applied to the current. A series resistance from a 100 µm thick layer, with a simple doping-dependent mobility μ₀/(1+√(N/10¹⁷)), is added; ohmic and neutral contacts are treated as ρc + Rs in series. Material constants are room-temperature textbook values (Sze, Ioffe database) and are approximate.

Model and assumptions

Band alignment follows Anderson's electron-affinity rule: ΔEC = χA − χB and ΔEV = (χA + EgA) − (χB + EgB). Real offsets often differ (Al₀.₃Ga₀.₇As/GaAs has ΔEC ≈ 0.65 ΔEg experimentally), so edit χ to impose a measured offset. The vacuum level is continuous unless you add an interface sheet charge.

Each side's bulk EF comes from charge neutrality with Boltzmann statistics and incomplete ionization, as in the contact tab. Poisson's equation is integrated exactly on each side from the interface into the bulk. The split of the total band bending between the two sides is found by requiring εBℰB(0⁺) − εAℰA(0⁻) = σint. At zero bias all carriers follow the common Fermi level. Under bias each side's majority carriers follow that side's quasi-Fermi level and minority carriers are held at their bulk density. The W values shown use the depletion approximation with the solved band bending on each side.

Current: electrons and holes each pass through a series combination of thermionic emission over the highest point of their band edge (A* = 120 m*/m₀) and minority-carrier diffusion into any side where they are minority (L = √(Dτ), capped at the 100 µm layer). This gives the Shockley diode for homojunctions, injection asymmetry for heterojunctions, and spike-limited transport when a conduction- or valence-band spike rises above both bulks. Depletion-region recombination q niW/2τ·(eqV/2kT − 1) is added for p–n junctions. Tunneling through spikes and band-to-band (Esaki) tunneling are not modeled. Lifetimes are typical textbook values: 1 µs for Si and Ge, 10 ns for most III–Vs, 1 ns for nitrides.

Model and assumptions

Ideal MOS capacitor: no gate leakage, no interface traps, and an optional fixed sheet charge Qf at the insulator/semiconductor interface. The gate equation is VG = Φms + ψs − (Qs + Qf)/Cox, so VFB = Φms − Qf/Cox. The semiconductor charge Qs(ψs) comes from the exact first integral of Poisson's equation with Boltzmann statistics and incomplete dopant ionization. Insulator band edges follow the electron-affinity rule; the oxide valence band is usually off scale.

VT is defined at ψs = 2φB, where qφB = |EF − Ei| in the bulk, and is computed from the exact Qs.

C–V curves: C = (1/Cox + 1/Cs)⁻¹. Low frequency uses Cs = |dQs/dψs| with all carriers responding. High frequency uses the same DC bias point, but the inversion charge cannot follow the AC signal, so Cs comes from majority carriers and dopants only. That makes C saturate at Cmin once ψs pins near 2φB. Deep depletion removes the inversion layer from the DC solution too, so the depletion region keeps widening past threshold. Quantum confinement and poly-gate depletion are not modeled, so very thin oxides look slightly better than they really are.
