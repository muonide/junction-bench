Model and assumptions

Bulk EF comes from charge neutrality p − n + ND+ − NA− = 0 with Boltzmann carrier statistics and incomplete dopant ionization (degeneracy factors 2 for donors, 4 for acceptors), so the dopant choice matters for deep levels such as Mg in GaN. Moving the Fermi-level slider solves backward for the doping that gives it. Poisson's equation is integrated exactly in 1D from the interface into the bulk (no depletion approximation), with the dopant ionization following the local band bending. The displayed W uses the depletion approximation W = √(2ε(Vbi − V − kT/q)/qN).

Barrier: qφBn = S(qφm − qχ) + (1 − S)(Eg − qφ0) (Cowley–Sze), with φ0 an approximate charge-neutrality level for each material. When S < 1 the vacuum level steps at the interface (interface dipole) and the metal sheet charge in Fig. 3 includes the interface-state charge. By default minority carriers are held at their bulk density (the usual Schottky-diode picture). With the minority-carrier option on, at zero bias they follow Boltzmann statistics too, which can produce an inversion layer under large barriers. Under bias the majority-carrier quasi-Fermi level stays flat in the semiconductor and minority carriers stay at their bulk density. Image-force lowering is reported but not drawn. Transport regime uses E00 = (qħ/2)√(N/m*ε): thermionic below 0.5 kT, thermionic-field to 2 kT, field emission (tunneling) above. The I–V curve (Fig. 5) uses the Tsu–Esaki form J = A*T² ∫ T(E) ln[(1+e^{(E_F,s−E)/kT})/(1+e^{(E_F,m−E)/kT})] dE/kT, with a WKB transmission T(E) through the parabolic depletion-approximation barrier, so thermionic, thermionic-field and field emission come out of one calculation; the dashed curve is thermionic emission alone. A** is the effective Richardson constant (112 A cm⁻² K⁻² for n-Si, 32 for p-Si, 8.2 for n-GaAs, 26 for n-GaN). Image-force lowering is not applied to the current. A series resistance from a 100 µm thick layer, with a Caughey–Thomas doping-dependent mobility (exact parameters for Si, a generic fit for other materials), is added; ohmic and neutral contacts are treated as ρc + Rs in series. Material constants are room-temperature textbook values (Sze, Ioffe database) and are approximate.

Model and assumptions

Band alignment uses the electron-affinity rule, ΔEC = χA − χB and ΔEV = (χA + EgA) − (χB + EgB), but the III–V affinities in this tab are effective values chosen to reproduce accepted band offsets (Vurgaftman, Meyer and Ram-Mohan 2001). For example, Al₀.₃Ga₀.₇As/GaAs gives ΔEC ≈ 0.22 eV and ΔEV ≈ 0.16 eV, InP/In₀.₅₃Ga₀.₄₇As gives 0.24/0.35 eV, and InAs/GaSb overlaps by about 0.2 eV. Edit χ to try other offsets. The vacuum level is continuous unless you add an interface sheet charge.

Each side's bulk EF comes from charge neutrality with Boltzmann statistics and incomplete ionization, as in the contact tab. Poisson's equation is integrated exactly on each side from the interface into the bulk. The split of the total band bending between the two sides is found by requiring εBℰB(0⁺) − εAℰA(0⁻) = σint. At zero bias all carriers follow the common Fermi level. Under bias each side's majority carriers follow that side's quasi-Fermi level and minority carriers are held at their bulk density. The W values shown use the depletion approximation with the solved band bending on each side.

Current: electrons and holes each pass through a series combination of thermionic emission over the highest point of their band edge (effective Richardson constants A**, e.g. 112 A cm⁻² K⁻² for n-Si, 32 for p-Si, 8.2 for n-GaAs) and minority-carrier diffusion into any side where they are minority (L = √(Dτ), capped at the 100 µm layer). This gives the Shockley diode for homojunctions, injection asymmetry for heterojunctions, and spike-limited transport when a conduction- or valence-band spike rises above both bulks. Depletion-region recombination q niW/2τ·(eqV/2kT − 1) is added for p–n junctions. Tunneling through spikes and band-to-band (Esaki) tunneling are not modeled. Lifetimes are typical textbook values: 1 µs for Si and Ge, 10 ns for most III–Vs, 1 ns for nitrides.

Model and assumptions

Ideal MOS capacitor: no gate leakage, no interface traps, and an optional fixed sheet charge Qf at the insulator/semiconductor interface. The gate equation is VG = Φms + ψs − (Qs + Qf)/Cox, so VFB = Φms − Qf/Cox. The semiconductor charge Qs(ψs) comes from the exact first integral of Poisson's equation with Boltzmann statistics and incomplete dopant ionization. Insulator band edges follow the electron-affinity rule; the oxide valence band is usually off scale.

VT is defined at ψs = 2φB, where qφB = |EF − Ei| in the bulk, and is computed from the exact Qs.

C–V curves: C = (1/Cox + 1/Cs)⁻¹. Low frequency uses Cs = |dQs/dψs| with all carriers responding. High frequency uses the same DC bias point, but the inversion charge cannot follow the AC signal, so Cs comes from majority carriers and dopants only. That makes C saturate at Cmin once ψs pins near 2φB. Deep depletion removes the inversion layer from the DC solution too, so the depletion region keeps widening past threshold. Quantum confinement and poly-gate depletion are not modeled, so very thin oxides look slightly better than they really are.

Model and assumptions

Materials. Composition-dependent 300 K parameters for AlxGa1−xAs (Γ and X valleys; X is the lowest band for x > 0.45), pseudomorphic InxGa1−xAs on GaAs, and Ga-face AlxGa1−xN and InxGa1−xN on GaN. Band alignments use valence-band offsets: GaAs/AlAs ΔEV = 0.53x eV (Vurgaftman 2001), with ΔEC = 0.65 ΔEg for InGaAs/GaAs and 0.7 ΔEg for the nitrides. Nitride polarization (spontaneous plus piezoelectric) uses linear fits that give σ/q ≈ 5.4×10¹³·x cm⁻² at AlGaN/GaN and ≈ 6.9×10¹³·x cm⁻² at InGaN/GaN. Strain shifts of the gaps and the light-hole band are not included.

Electrostatics. Poisson's equation is solved by Newton iteration with finite volumes on a mesh refined to 0.1 nm at every interface. It uses Fermi–Dirac statistics (Aymerich-Humet approximation to F1/2), incomplete ionization, and bound polarization charge −dP/dz. The surface is either a Schottky contact (EC = EF,gate + qφB) or ohmic. The substrate end is a neutral ohmic contact.

Quantum region. In layers marked Q, electrons fill 2D subbands, Ei found from the BenDaniel–Duke effective-mass equation on the Γ band with hard walls at the region's edges. Each subband holds ni = (m*kT/πħ²)·ln(1+e(EF−Ei)/kT). Electrons above the region's lowest edge are counted classically. Schrödinger and Poisson are iterated to self-consistency with a predictor–corrector scheme. Heavy-hole states are computed on the final potential but are not fed back into Poisson.

Bias. In p–n structures the electron and hole quasi-Fermi levels are taken as flat at their contact values. This is accurate when wide-gap cladding layers block minority injection, as in LEDs and lasers, and approximate for homojunctions. In n–i–n structures the quasi-Fermi level is interpolated linearly across the undoped region. Tunneling current uses the Tsu–Esaki formula with transfer-matrix transmission through the quantum region, resolving each resonance adaptively. It assumes coherent transport (no scattering), so valley currents are underestimated.
